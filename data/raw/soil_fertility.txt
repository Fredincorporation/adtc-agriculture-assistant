Soil Fertility Management for Smallholder Farmers

Why soil fertility declines:
- Continuous cropping without returning nutrients
- Erosion
- Burning of crop residues
- Low use of manure or compost

Low-cost ways to improve soil:

1. Farmyard Manure and Compost
- Collect animal manure, crop residues, kitchen waste, and ashes.
- Make compost by layering green materials (nitrogen) and dry materials (carbon).
- Keep the heap moist and turn it every 2–3 weeks.
- Compost is ready in 1.5–3 months when it is dark and crumbly.

2. Intercropping and Crop Rotation
- Grow legumes (beans, groundnuts, cowpeas, soybeans) with cereals.
- Rotate maize with legumes or cassava.
- This adds nitrogen and reduces pests.

3. Mulching
- Cover soil with crop residues or grasses.
- Reduces erosion, keeps moisture, and adds organic matter as it decomposes.

4. Avoid burning crop residues
- Leave residues on the field or compost them.

5. Micro-dose fertilizer (if available)
- Apply small amounts of fertilizer near the plant instead of broadcasting. This increases efficiency and reduces cost.

Healthy soil = higher yields with less money spent on external inputs.
