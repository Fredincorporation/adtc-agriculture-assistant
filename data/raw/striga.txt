Striga (Witchweed) Management

What it is:
- A parasitic weed that attaches to the roots of cereals (maize, sorghum, millet) and rice.
- Causes severe yield loss, especially on poor soils.

How to recognise it:
- Small plants with bright flowers (often purple or pink) appearing in the field.
- Cereal plants look stunted and yellow even when soil moisture seems adequate.

Control methods:
1. Improve soil fertility — Striga is worse on poor soils. Use manure and compost.
2. Rotate with non-host crops (cassava, legumes, sweet potato).
3. Hand weeding before Striga produces seed.
4. Use trap crops or catch crops where recommended.
5. Some resistant or tolerant maize and sorghum varieties exist — use them if available.
6. Avoid moving soil or manure from heavily infested fields.

Early action and better soil fertility are the most practical approaches for most smallholders.
