Basic Livestock Health Care for Smallholder Farmers

General good practices:
- Provide clean water at all times.
- Give adequate feed and mineral supplements when possible.
- Keep housing clean, dry and well ventilated.
- Separate sick animals from the rest of the herd when possible.
- Practice regular observation — look at appetite, walking, breathing, dung and general behaviour every day.

Common issues and first responses:

1. Diarrhoea
- Ensure clean water and clean environment.
- Offer good quality roughage.
- If the animal is weak or many animals are affected, seek professional help.

2. Wounds and skin problems
- Clean the area with clean water and mild soap or salt solution.
- Keep the wound clean and protect it from flies.
- Serious or deep wounds need professional attention.

3. Respiratory signs (coughing, difficult breathing)
- Reduce dust and smoke in the housing.
- Provide shelter from cold wind and rain.
- Seek help if the condition is severe or spreading.

4. External parasites (ticks, lice, mites)
- See separate guidance on tick control.
- Regular inspection is the most important step.

Important principles:
- Prevention is cheaper than cure.
- Do not give human medicines or random chemicals to animals.
- When in doubt, contact a local veterinary officer, animal health worker or extension agent.
- Keep simple records of treatments and animal movements if possible.
