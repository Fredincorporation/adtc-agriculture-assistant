Simple Market Advisory for Smallholder Farmers

Before producing for the market:
- Know who will buy your produce (local market, traders, schools, processors).
- Understand the quality and quantity buyers expect.
- Estimate the cost of production versus the likely selling price.

Timing of sales:
- Prices are often lower at peak harvest when many farmers sell at once.
- Storing properly (when possible) can allow sales later when prices improve.
- For perishable vegetables, plan production so harvest matches better demand periods.

Reducing risk:
- Do not depend on only one buyer.
- Avoid taking large loans based on optimistic price assumptions.
- Keep part of the harvest for household food security.

Quality and presentation:
- Clean, well-dried, and well-graded produce usually fetches better prices.
- Sort out damaged or diseased items before selling.
- For some crops, simple packaging or bulk arrangement improves attractiveness to buyers.

Collecting market information:
- Talk regularly to local traders and other farmers.
- Visit the market occasionally to observe prices and demand.
- Ask extension officers if they have market information.

Transport and costs:
- Calculate the cost of getting produce to market (transport, loading, time).
- Sometimes selling at the farm gate for a slightly lower price is better after costs are considered.

Good market decisions start with knowing your costs and understanding what buyers actually want.
