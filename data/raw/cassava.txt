Cassava Production and Problem Management for African Smallholders

Planting:
- Use healthy stem cuttings 20–30 cm long with at least 5–7 nodes.
- Plant at the beginning of the rainy season.
- Spacing: 1 m × 1 m (or 1 m × 0.8 m in fertile soils).
- Plant cuttings at an angle or horizontally, 10–15 cm deep.

Soil and Fertility:
- Cassava tolerates poor soils but responds well to manure and compost.
- Avoid waterlogging. Choose well-drained fields.
- Intercrop with legumes (groundnut, beans, cowpea) in the first few months.

Major Problems:

1. Cassava Mosaic Disease (CMD)
- Leaves become yellow/mosaic, plants are stunted.
- Control: Use resistant varieties, clean planting material, remove infected plants early, control whiteflies.

2. Cassava Brown Streak Disease (CBSD)
- Causes root rot and yellowing on leaves.
- Control: Resistant varieties, clean cuttings, early harvesting in some cases.

3. Cassava Mealybug and Green Mite
- Cause leaf damage and yield loss.
- Control: Natural enemies (release of parasitoids where available), avoid excessive pesticide use, maintain field hygiene.

Harvesting:
- Most varieties are ready from 8–18 months depending on type and location.
- Harvest carefully to avoid damaging roots.
