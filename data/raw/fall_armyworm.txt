Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) Control for African Smallholder Farmers

Identification:
- Young larvae are light green with dark heads.
- Older larvae have inverted "Y" mark on the head and four dark spots forming a square on the second-to-last segment.
- Prefer maize, sorghum, and millet.

Low-cost Control Methods:

1. Early Detection (Most Important)
- Scout fields early in the morning or late afternoon.
- Look for windowing (transparent patches) on leaves and frass (droppings) in the whorl.
- Check 20 plants per field at least twice a week during vegetative stage.

2. Hand Picking and Destruction
- For light infestations, remove and crush larvae by hand.
- Destroy heavily damaged plants or parts.
- Do this early in the morning when larvae are active.

3. Neem-based Solutions
- Collect fresh neem leaves or seeds.
- Pound 1 kg of neem leaves/seeds and soak in 5 litres of water overnight.
- Strain and add a small amount of soap as sticker.
- Spray early morning or late evening. Repeat every 5–7 days.
- Neem acts as antifeedant and growth regulator.

4. Ash and Soil Methods
- Apply wood ash or fine soil into the maize whorl. This desiccates young larvae.
- Mix ash with a little chili powder for better effect.

5. Cultural Practices
- Plant early at the onset of rains.
- Avoid staggered planting in the same area.
- Intercrop with beans, cassava, or non-host crops.
- Rotate maize with non-host crops (e.g. cassava, groundnuts, beans).
- Keep fields free of weeds that can host the pest.

6. Natural Enemies
- Encourage birds, spiders, earwigs, and parasitic wasps.
- Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides that kill beneficial insects.

7. When to Use Chemical Control
- Only if infestation exceeds 20% of plants and larvae are still small.
- Prefer selective products if available and follow label rates strictly.
- Never use banned or highly toxic chemicals.

Prevention is better than cure. Focus on monitoring and cultural methods first.
