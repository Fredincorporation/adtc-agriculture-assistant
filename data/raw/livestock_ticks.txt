Tick and External Parasite Control for Smallholder Livestock (Cattle, Goats, Sheep)

Why ticks are a problem:
- Cause blood loss, stress, reduced weight gain and milk production.
- Transmit serious diseases (e.g. East Coast fever, anaplasmosis, heartwater in some regions).

Low-cost and practical control methods:

1. Regular inspection
- Check animals at least twice a week, especially around the ears, neck, dewlap, udder, under the tail and between the legs.
- Early detection makes control much easier.

2. Manual removal
- For light infestations, carefully remove ticks by hand.
- Grasp the tick close to the skin and pull steadily upward. Do not twist or crush the tick on the animal.
- Kill removed ticks (e.g. by dropping into strong alcohol or soapy water) and dispose of them safely.

3. Improve animal and environmental hygiene
- Keep the kraal or night enclosure clean and dry.
- Remove tall grass and bushes around the kraal.
- Avoid overgrazing and try to rotate grazing areas when possible.

4. Local and botanical options
- Some farmers use neem extracts, diluted soap solutions, or other local plants. Results vary and these methods usually need frequent application.
- Always test a small area of skin first and observe the animal.

5. Conventional acaricides (when available and affordable)
- Use only products recommended for livestock.
- Follow the label dosage and safety instructions strictly.
- Do not use crop pesticides or products meant for dogs on cattle or goats.
- Observe withdrawal periods for milk and meat.

6. When to seek help
- If animals show fever, swollen lymph nodes, difficulty breathing, or many animals are affected, contact a veterinary officer or animal health worker quickly.

Prevention through regular checking and clean housing is more effective and cheaper than treating heavy infestations.
